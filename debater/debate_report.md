**Summary of positions**  
Pro‑LLM: LLMs generate sizable productivity gains (e.g., 28 % faster coding, 15 % higher math scores) and spur large‑scale enterprise adoption, promising a $13 trillion GDP boost by 2030.  
Anti‑LLM: The same gains are offset by reliability failures, displacement risks, market concentration, and substantial energy costs that erode net benefit.

**Strongest pro‑LLM argument**  
Empirical field trials (Microsoft Copilot, Khanmigo) show measurable time‑saving and learning‑outcome improvements, directly linking LLM use to higher output and human‑capital development.

**Strongest anti‑LLM argument**  
Concrete failure cases (unsafe medical dosage, legal liability waiver) illustrate that hallucinations are not merely academic; verification overhead can nullify reported productivity gains, especially in high‑stakes domains.

**Key weaknesses**  
- Pro side downplays error‑correction time and assumes seamless human‑in‑the‑loop oversight, which may be unrealistic at scale.  
- Anti side overstates the magnitude of displacement without evidence that reskilling programs can meet the pace of automation, and it treats all energy use as net negative without comparing to baseline ICT consumption.

**Evidence quality assessment**  
Both sides cite recent, reputable sources (Microsoft internal study, Gartner survey, World Economic Forum, TruthfulQA benchmark). However, the pro arguments rely on pilot‑scale metrics that may not extrapolate to enterprise‑wide settings, while the anti arguments lean heavily on isolated incidents and macro‑level estimates (energy per model) lacking cost‑benefit context.

**Unresolved questions**  
1. How much additional time is required for verification in real‑world deployments, and does it offset the 28 % coding speed gain?  
2. What is the net carbon footprint when LLM inference is amortized across productivity benefits?  
3. Can reskilling pipelines reliably absorb 25 % of displaced tasks within the cited 12–18 month window?

**Final assessment**  
LLMs demonstrably boost specific productivity and learning outcomes, but the net economic impact remains contingent on error mitigation, verification costs, and sustainability practices. The debate hinges on quantifying these hidden costs; until they are rigorously measured, the claim that benefits “far outweigh” risks is plausible but not yet conclusively proven.
